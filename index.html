A personal project, shared with friends.

Ledger started with a problem I kept running into myself: in an age where almost every payment is digital, money leaves your pocket invisibly. You tap, you pay, and you rarely get a real sense of how much you're spending or where it's actually going.

Tracking your daily expenses fixes that. It gives you back the awareness that cash used to force on us — a clear grip on the small, irrelevant spends that quietly drain away without you noticing.

But here's the part that matters most to me: this isn't about depriving yourself. You don't have to become a penny-pinching freak who never spends a dime on yourself or the people you love. It's the opposite. Managing your money well means that when you do spend, you spend deliberately — you know exactly where it's going, and how much of your budget is left this month. You spend where it truly counts: on yourself, your family, your loved ones.

Morgan Housel's The Psychology of Money — the first book that started my own financial education — makes a point I keep coming back to: doing well with money has less to do with how smart you are and far more to do with how you behave. Real wealth is often the spending you don't see, and learning when you have "enough" is a quiet superpower. — paraphrasing ideas from Morgan Housel, The Psychology of Money

Financial management is one of the oldest and most misunderstood skills there is. My mission with Ledger is to help others reach that same level of confidence in their spending — to be financially smart in an era built to make us spend the next dime, where every ad is engineered to reach into your pocket. The goal is to be the master of your money, not a slave to careless, unknowing spending.

✨ Coming soon: Savings plans

The next big thing we're building: Ledger will study your spending style — where and how you spend the most — find the weak spots where money could be saved, and help you build an effective plan toward your next big purchase.

I'm always open to suggestions and I genuinely listen to criticism. This is built for you — tell me how to make it better.
